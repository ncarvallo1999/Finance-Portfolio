## Sizing

- Trade 1 was allowed $200 of risk. The position cap limited it to $59.16.
- After the win, the next trade's risk would have been 2% of $10,118 + 50% × $118 ≈ **$261**.
- No second trade happened. COST closed at 11:15, but no check ran between 11:15 and the 11:30 entry cutoff.

## Caveats

- **Fills:** the model assumes fills exactly at the planned prices, with no slippage. A real market order at 10:12 and a limit fill at the target could differ by a few cents a share.
- **Ties:** a bar touching both the stop and the target would have counted as a stop. That didn't happen.
- **Entry timing:** the entry used the 10:12 price, when the check actually ran, not the earlier 9:50 breakout price. That avoids hindsight.
- **Sample size:** one trade proves nothing about the strategy.

## Lessons

1. **The check-ins fired 20–40 minutes late.** That cost the 9:50 entry and left the 11:15–11:30 window unchecked. Fix: run a polling loop in the session, such as `entry.py watch`, instead of relying on one-shot reminders.
2. **The pre-market thesis failed at the open.** The CEO-exit momentum names reversed and money rotated into defensives. Checking the whole market instead of only the shortlist is what found a trade. Keep doing both.
3. **Low-volatility stocks hit the position cap before the risk limit.** COST risked only 0.3% a share, so $19,000 of exposure used just 30% of the $200 risk budget. To use the full risk budget, favour stocks with wider ranges or allow two positions at once.
4. **The replay filter helped.** It rejected ABT and approved COST. Keep requiring a positive average R.
5. **Next time:** use the same rules, poll inside the session from 9:45 to 11:30 ET, and allow up to two concurrent positions within the $19,000 cap.
